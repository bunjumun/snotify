Visualizer FX Settings

Projector
Not projecting.
Only the picture goes to the projector, fullscreen. This panel, the readout and the buttons stay on this screen, and this screen shows a mirror of what the audience sees. Set the projector to Extend, not Mirror, in System Settings, Displays. The first time, Chrome asks to manage windows on your displays. The audience's controller plugs into this laptop. Audience control (J, or the button top right, or MIDI) decides whether it is heard: off, the controller is ignored while you play from the keyboard or MIDI; on, it drives the projector. Escape on the projector, or the 📺 button, brings the picture back.
Effects
Colour, glitch and cycling
Limit what the controller can reach
The controller steps effects with the stick clicks (R3, L3), and the auto-cycle only moves between ticked ones. Tick a single effect to hold the show on it: the buttons then simply do nothing, with no message. Picking an effect above, or with the number keys, always works for you.
Each slider is the resting value; the thin bar under it is where the sound and the sticks have actually pushed it. Choosing an effect by hand pauses the auto-cycle (on the beat when audio is playing, every 45 seconds when nothing is).
Camera layer
Camera is off.
With the camera on, the right stick (the arrow keys on a keyboard) moves the mix: up toward all camera, down toward all animations, faster the further you hold it. It stays where you leave it when the stick goes back to centre. It comes on at half and half, and the 50% button puts it back there. The effects work on the mix. The camera is only started from here, with K, or from the camera button, never by the controller. The picture is processed on this page and never uploaded.
Movement
What the camera sees moving shows here.
The camera is aimed at a stage the projector is lighting, so it watches for movement rather than light, and lifts the band out of the picture. Auto balance takes the stage as it is lit now as the background and sets the noise floor from two seconds of the show's own flicker; Learn background does just the first part (use it when the stage is empty). A person who stands still melts into the background after the learning time, and moving again lights them back up. Sensitivity and the noise floor decide how big a change counts; Trail leaves a shape behind a moving body; Lift and Stillness dimmed decide how hard the moving parts stand out; Outline draws the edge of the moving shape. Movement is also a source for the routes on the Sound tab, so it can drive the effect, and Movement adds colour and zoom sets how much a moving band lends the picture a little saturation and a slight push in. Camera position dynamics sets how much where it moves steers the effect: left and right rotate it, up and down rescale it, and the push in leans toward the movement (the ring in the preview is where it thinks that is). 0 turns the position off. All of it gives itself back once everything is still.
Picture
Auto levels reads the camera's current frame and sets these: black and white points from its darkest and brightest parts, colour balance so the average colour is neutral, and gamma so the middle tones land where the effects work best. It suits a steady scene; on a stage lit by the projector, use the movement controls instead. Everything stays adjustable by hand. Set the effect to RAW to see the camera on its own while you dial it in.

Tilt sensors
Tilt sensors not connected.
Automated mode: with nobody on the controls for that long (a controller being ignored counts as nobody), the rotate, scale, split, push and warp drift on their own, and the camera mix wanders around the middle, until the controls are touched again. Set it to 0 to turn it off.

Rumble: the bass drives the big motor, the highs the small one, and a beat adds a thump. Only while the controller is being listened to and the sound is playing, never in silence. It needs Chrome and an Xbox, DualShock or DualSense pad. Tilt uses the motion sensors in a DualShock 4 or DualSense, plugged in with a USB cable (Bluetooth carries no motion data, and Xbox pads have none). Tilting left and right rotates the effect and tilting forward and back rescales it, added to the left stick. Hold the controller the way it will be held and press Level; if a direction feels backwards, flip it.
Mix
Tap in time to set the pulse's tempo, with or without audio.
Decks
The scenes no longer follow the sticks: the controller belongs to the effect, and the scenes are moved by the sound and by what is routed to them. The Visualizer's five scenes take every audio route; the mixer's six (Tunnel, Plasma, Stars, Waves, Grid, Vortex) follow the sound through their own reactions.
Audio Input
Not connected: it drifts on its own until you play it.
Device names stay hidden until you grant mic access once — hit Connect, allow, then pick your real device and connect again. Route a DAW bus in through BlackHole, Loopback, or any virtual audio device to drive this per channel.
Live Channels
Solo a track in your DAW to find which channel it lands on.
Modulation Routing
The party map is already loaded: bass pushes the effect's amount, warp and threshold and swells the scene; mids breathe the scale and complexity; highs drive the RGB and CMYK shift and the glow. Mix means the loudest channel, so it works on any input. A negative depth pulls a parameter down instead of up.
Response
Controller feel is how much the controller is smoothed: low is snappy, high is flowing, like something with weight. Pulse is how hard the sound hits the picture: 0 ignores it, 1.5 lets it throw everything about. Demo pulse is an optional stand-in for audio, so you can see what the map does with nothing connected; it is off by default and with nothing playing the tool is ambient. Auto level learns each band's own floor and peak, so the same mapping works from a quiet mic or a hot DAW bus. Idle motion sets how alive it is with no input at all. Beat threshold is how far the bass must jump above its own average to count as a beat: higher means fewer beats.
